## Problem Statement

Revelio has a working product (8-question free-text reflection → 3 personalized career paths) deployed at revelio-me.vercel.app, recently adapted for the 16–18 student audience and bilingual (English/Spanish). The product is not the bottleneck. The validation doc identified the bottleneck honestly: **zero sales conversations have happened**. The next 30–60 days must turn structural analysis into real customer evidence — payment, behavior, or rejection — through paths that match the founder's actual situation (solo, Madrid-based, no education-industry connections, but one warm relationship at a former school).

## Demand Evidence

- **Hypothetical / structural only at session start:** Schools pay $2.52–$10/student/year for inferior incumbents (Naviance, Scoir, MaiaLearning, Unifrog, BridgeU). IECs charge families $5K–$50K per student. The category exists.
- **Real evidence emerged during the session:** Founder has a warm relationship with the teacher at Runnymede College who organizes alumni speaker sessions (founder is an alumnus and has spoken to current students). This is a concrete, named, reachable contact. The validation doc claimed "no education connections" — the session surfaced that this was understated.
- **Synthetic user testing** (paralyzed high school senior persona) confirmed product friction points but is not demand evidence. Real students engaging with the product under real conditions remains untested.
- **Zero paying customers, zero pilot agreements** — confirmed and unchanged at session end.

## Status Quo

- **Schools currently use:** Naviance / Scoir (application logistics, not career discovery), Unifrog / BridgeU (course and university browsing), MaiaLearning (planning), YouScience (aptitude tests). Frequent counselor complaint per [Reddit r/schoolcounseling](https://www.reddit.com/r/schoolcounseling/comments/1pitpm0/honest_thoughts_on_naviancescoir_etc/) and the validation doc: these tools handle *what to apply to*, not *what to actually want*. PowerSchool/Naviance settled a $17.25M class-action in late February 2026 over student data tracking ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/what-the-1725m-naviance-settlement-means-for-school-districts/816496/)) — schools are in a defensive posture on new external tools that handle student data.
- **Parents currently use:** Spreadsheets, Reddit, peer parent groups, and (for the affluent) IECs at $5K–$50K. Many parents do not know IECs exist; many cannot afford one.
- **Students currently do:** Drift. Pick a major based on parents' suggestions, family pressure, or what their friends pick. Most cannot articulate what they want when asked directly.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Track 1 (B2B, warm):** First buyer = Runnymede College, Madrid, via the alumni-organizer teacher contact. Pilot scope: one Sixth Form group (~20–30 students), free, before summer break 2026. Goal: product validation + testimonial + observation, not pricing validation.

**Track 2 (B2C, parallel):** First buyers = parents of 16–18 students in US/UK/Spain who are anxious about their child's post-school direction and have $39–$79 of discretionary spend. Reached via Meta ads on a paid landing page variant. Goal: prove someone unrelated to the founder will pay.

**Narrowest possible version of each:**
- Track 1: Founder + teacher + one cohort + manual report. Zero new code.
- Track 2: Stripe Checkout button on a landing page variant, $500 ad budget, 14-day window. Minor copy + payment integration; no B2B features.

## Constraints

- **Solo founder, day job.** Realistic budget for this work: ~10–15 hours/week. That is the binding constraint on everything below. Track 1 + Track 2 fit *only* because Track 2 has been deliberately scoped down (organic posts, no ads, no full launch) — see Track 2 below.
- **Madrid-based** — geographic asset for in-person Madrid school visits, language asset for Spanish-curriculum schools, time-zone friction for US international schools.
- **No prior education-industry network** beyond Runnymede.
- **Funded by founder's day job** at Spotify — pace must be sustainable, not heroic.
- **Procurement timing:** April–May 2026 is the wrong window for 2026–27 institutional budgets. This is why Track 1 is structured as a *free pre-summer pilot*, with paid renewal/expansion in the 2026–27 academic year.
- **Track 1 calendar pressure:** late May–late June 2026 overlaps Spanish school exam season (selectividad / IB final exams). Schools may be unable to schedule a pilot before summer break. Honest acknowledgment: a fall 2026 pilot is plausibly the realistic outcome; September–October becomes the fallback window.

## Premises

After premise challenge with founder pushback on 3 of 6 original premises:

1. **Product is not the bottleneck — sales conversation count is.** The product is built, adapted for students, bilingual, with STT. Every further *student-facing feature* is procrastination unless a paying customer asked for it. **Carve-out:** Track 2's payment + landing-page work is *channel infrastructure*, not product features — the same product is delivered, only the buying flow is new. This carve-out is intentional and bounded; it does not extend to new student-facing functionality.
2. **Revelio is in a different problem space from Naviance, Unifrog, BridgeU.** They handle *application logistics* and *browsing*. Revelio answers *what do I actually want to study? do I even need to go to college?* The pitch should be "use this *before* you open Naviance," not "instead of."
3. **Cold global outreach is dominated by warm local intro for school #1.** A warm intro to one Madrid international school via a known teacher is faster, higher-conversion, and learning-richer than 20 cold emails to schools in 6 countries.
4. **B2C parents is an unexplored angle worth testing in parallel.** Parents already pay $5K–$50K for IECs. $39–$79 is frictionless. The question is reachability and conversion, answerable in 14 days with $500.
5. **The first warm-intro pilot can be free; school #2 onward must be paid.** First pilot validates *product*. Paid pilots validate *willingness to pay*. The discipline of charging starts at school #2, not school #1.
6. **Bespoke feature requests get logged, not built.** A feature ships only when 3 schools (or N parents at meaningful scale) have asked for the same thing. Otherwise the founder becomes a custom-software shop for one school.

Premises explicitly considered and rejected during the session:
- *"Sell to Heads of Wellbeing"* — the title doesn't reliably exist; let the inside contact at Runnymede route the warm intro to the right person.
- *"IECs are the wedge"* — most IECs operate around prestige admissions; their incentive structure is misaligned with Revelio's "what do you actually want" value proposition.
- *"Gatsby Benchmarks framing"* — relevant for UK state-funded schools, largely irrelevant for international private schools.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: The validation doc's plan (cold outreach, sharpened)
Send 20 hand-crafted cold emails/LinkedIn messages to private and international school decision-makers globally, pitched on the new "different problem from Naviance" positioning. Pilot at $500–$2,500 per cohort.
- **Effort:** M · **Risk:** High
- **Pros:** Aligns with existing analysis. Established budget category. Defensible pricing.
- **Cons:** ~7.8% EdTech reply rate × 20 = 1–2 conversations (small learning loop). Wrong window for 2026–27 budgets. Pure cold with zero warm intros.
- **Reuses:** Existing app, existing Vercel deployment.

### Approach B: B2C parents wedge (single-bet)
Stripe Checkout at $39–$79 for individual student access. Meta ads to "parents of 16–18 high schoolers" in US/UK/Spain. 14 days, $500 ad budget. Single question answered: *will parents pay?*
- **Effort:** S · **Risk:** Medium (paid ads can be inefficient at low budget)
- **Pros:** 14-day feedback loop. Single-decision-maker buyer. No B2B features required. Generates conversion data that strengthens later B2B pitches.
- **Cons:** Brand positioning shifts toward "consumer tool." Customer support volume scales with parent count. Product currently has no payment integration.
- **Reuses:** Existing app, Vercel deployment, existing landing page (with copy variant).

### Approach C: Madrid geo-wedge, in-person
Three in-person visits per week to international schools in the Madrid metro area (~50 schools). Bilingual + local-presence differentiator. Aim for one verbal yes by June, pilot in October 2026.
- **Effort:** M · **Risk:** Medium
- **Pros:** In-person closes 10–50× better than email. Founder's home market and language. Local referrals possible.
- **Cons:** Small market sample. Same procurement timing problem as Approach A. Calendar-intensive vs digital outreach.
- **Reuses:** Founder's local presence, existing app.

### Approach D (chosen — sharper than what the office hours partner generated): Warm-intro Runnymede pilot + parallel small-scope B2C parents test
Track 1: Warm intro message to the alumni-organizer teacher at Runnymede College this week, proposing a free pilot — best case before summer break, realistic case fall 2026 — with one Sixth Form cohort. Track 2: Build Stripe Checkout + parent landing page variant on a separate branch, set price via `/minimalist-entrepreneur:pricing`, then post organically in 3–5 parent communities — **no paid ads in v1.** Day-21 decision gate (anchored to first organic post): whichever track produced the stronger forward-looking revenue signal — a verbal commit for a 2026–27 paid cohort, or paying parents — double down on.

## Recommended Approach: Approach D

**Why D wins over A, B, or C alone:**
- A is dominated by D-Track-1: the warm Runnymede intro is strictly better than cold global outreach for school #1.
- B alone gives up the institutional optionality that Track 1 preserves at near-zero cost.
- C is good in principle but slower than D-Track-1 (you don't need three Madrid schools to learn — you need one cohort actually using the product, which Track 1 delivers faster).
- D's parallel structure works because Track 1's manual workload is contained (one cohort, one report) and Track 2's manual workload is small (Stripe + organic posts + iteration — no ads). Both can be carried by one person with a day job.

**What would have to be true for D to be wrong:**
- Runnymede teacher contact is unresponsive or no longer there (verify before relying on this single point of failure).
- Track 2 produces zero conversions on organic posts AND Track 1 cohort produces zero student engagement — in which case the underlying value prop, not the channel, is the issue.

### Track 1 — Runnymede warm pilot

**Outreach strategy: share the product, extract pain + willingness-to-pay in one message.** A 30-minute meeting ask is too heavy. But a *too-short* question without context lands as a cold ping. The right balance: a single concise message that (a) shares Revelio directly so she can try it or pass to students, (b) explains what it is, who it's for, and the philosophy behind it, (c) asks 1–2 targeted questions designed to extract both the hair-on-fire pain and the price tolerance for solving it. The link goes upfront — no "demo later" gating.

#### Outreach phase

1. **Send the warm-intro message** to the alumni-organizer teacher by Friday May 8, 2026. **In English** (Runnymede is a British international school). Structure (~10–12 lines):
   - Warm anchor (reference last year's alumni session, a specific memory)
   - **What Revelio is**: free 8-question reflection tool for 16–18s, free-text answers (not dropdowns), AI generates 3 personalized career paths
   - **Who it's for + philosophy**: built for students who don't know what they want to study or do — answers the question that comes *before* "which college do I apply to," not the same one
   - **Personal anchor**: built it after watching nieces struggle with exactly this
   - **Direct invitation to try**: link upfront (revelio-me.vercel.app), no login, ~15 min, share with any students who might benefit
   - **Two targeted questions:**
     1. *"When you work with Sixth Form students who don't know what they want to do after school, what's the biggest pain point you face — yours, theirs, or the school's?"*
     2. *"If a tool like Revelio genuinely helped solve that, what would have to be true for Runnymede to consider paying for it — say, in the £5–£10/student/year range?"*
   - Sign-off: *"Honest answers, even short ones, would be incredibly useful."*

   **Why these questions:** Q1 surfaces the pain point in her words. Q2 is the WTP anchor — by naming a specific price band, you force a concrete reaction (push back, validate, or explain what would change the math) instead of "interesting, we'll think about it." This is willingness-to-pay extraction by design.

2. **If she engages substantively** (answers either question, even briefly, or has students try it): thank her, then ask for a 30-minute call to discuss running it with a cohort. The 30-min ask is now appropriate because the relationship has moved past cold-message threshold.

3. **If she replies short, polite but non-committal:** that's its own signal — schools may not have the WTP conversation yet, or she may not be the right person to have it with. Send Revelio to her anyway with a low-pressure "feel free to share with whoever this would be most useful for at Runnymede." Re-engage in 4–6 weeks with one update.

4. **If no reply within 7 days:** send a one-line follow-up only — *"no rush, just bumping in case the first one got buried."* Then move to Track 1 fallback (Approach C — Madrid in-person) for school #1.

#### Pilot phase (only if outreach produced a yes)

5. **Prepare a one-page privacy/data note** *before* the pilot scheduling meeting. Content: for the school pilot, **Revelio acts as data processor; the school is the data controller** (school decides purpose and instructs processing — Revelio executes). Free-text answers are sent to Google Gemini API for inference, governed by Google's [API data use policy](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms) — not used for model training under the API tier; no PII required (students can use first name only); session data deletable on request; no third-party tracking pixels. This framing matters legally — the prior version's "founder is the data controller" was wrong for school pilots and would create unwarranted liability.
6. **Confirm consent path with the school.** Minors' data: confirm whether Runnymede has blanket parental consent for educational tools or whether opt-in consent is needed per cohort. Defer to school's existing process — do not invent one. If the school wants a Data Processing Agreement, **read it before signing** — flag clauses around indemnification, audit rights, breach notification windows, and sub-processor restrictions. A €100–€300 one-hour review by a Spanish/EU data-privacy lawyer is cheap insurance and worth it for the first DPA, even unpaid pilot.
7. **Define the pilot observation rubric** before the cohort runs:
   - Engagement (automated): % of students who completed all 8 questions; average word count per answer
   - Engagement (manual): % of student submissions tagged "thoughtful" by founder on review — budget ~30 min per cohort of 30 (~10 hours for a 20–30 student cohort if read carefully; less if just spot-checking word count + obvious copy-paste). Acknowledge: this is a real time cost, not a checkbox.
   - Output quality: counselor rates each student's 3 paths on a 1–5 "useful / not useful" scale
   - Counselor reaction: 15-minute exit interview, recorded with consent, on (a) what students said about the experience, (b) what the counselor would change, (c) whether they would use it next year and at what price
8. **Run the pilot.** Founder is in the room or on a call when students use it — observation, not survey. **PTO note:** Sixth Form sessions run during Spotify work hours. Plan to take 4–6 hours of PTO (one half-day during the pilot, plus the exit interview slot). Block this on the calendar at meeting time; do not assume it will fit around work.
9. **Compile the report manually.** Themes, standout paths, what students said in their own words, the counselor's exit interview transcript.
10. **Fallback if school leadership blocks the teacher's yes:** Convert the relationship into an introduction. Ask the teacher: *"Who at Runnymede would I need to convince? Could you introduce me?"* — this preserves the warm thread even if the pilot itself can't happen at Runnymede this term.
11. **Convert the testimonial** into pitch material for schools #2+ in the 2026–27 academic year — this is when paid pricing discipline begins.

### Track 2 — B2C parents experiment (deliberately small-scope)
**Budget discipline: $0–$100 for v1, no ads as primary channel.** Meta ads at $35/day produce noise below their learning threshold; even $500 wouldn't return clean signal. Organic posts in well-chosen parent communities cost $0 and produce more honest signal — comments, objections, real reactions. **Optional escape hatch:** if organic gets zero engagement after week 1 (no comments, no upvotes, no DMs), spend $50–$100 boosting one already-written Reddit post in a single subreddit (Reddit Promoted Posts, ~$5–$50 CPM, less noisy than Meta at low spend). Total Track 2 ad spend ceiling: $100. If $100 produces nothing, the channel mix is wrong — do not graduate to bigger ads on this signal.

**Define the SKU first (before pricing skill, before Stripe).** What does the parent actually buy? Recommendation: **one-time purchase of "Revelio for one student" — student gets unlimited access to refine and revisit their answers for 12 months, parent gets the final 3-path report by email at completion.** This is a single-purchase, single-student SKU. No subscriptions in v1; no parent dashboard; no multi-child bundle. The implementer needs this single sentence locked in before opening Stripe.

**Bilingual decision:** Ship `/parent` in English first. Add Spanish only after the English version produces a paying parent. Don't translate copy that hasn't converted in either language yet.

1. **Run `/minimalist-entrepreneur:pricing`** on the SKU above. Output should be a single recommended price + one alternate. **Decision rule if it returns a range:** pick the median of the recommended range as the launch price. Do not A/B test on $0 ad spend.
2. **Build Stripe Checkout integration** in a separate branch. Use **Stripe-hosted Checkout** (minimal Stripe-provided terms, not custom ToS) and **Stripe Tax** (configure once: enable in Stripe Dashboard, register VAT/tax IDs as required by your jurisdiction — note: this is *not* fully automatic, it's a 1–2 hour setup). Add Resend (or whatever transactional email is already wired — verify in `server/` first) for receipt + report-delivery email. Add a one-paragraph refund policy on the landing page ("14-day refund, no questions asked"). ~16–20 hours of focused work including tax setup.
3. **Add a parent-facing landing page variant** at `/parent`. Keep the existing student-targeted page intact for Track 1. Update root navigation: existing root stays as-is (student audience), `/parent` is a separate route accessed only via direct link or campaign URL — no navigation changes that affect Runnymede pilot traffic. Use `/direct-response-copy` skill (loaded) for the hero + CTA. ~6 hours.
4. **Post organically** in 3–5 parent communities: r/Parenting, r/HighSchool (parent-adjacent threads), Facebook "high school parents" groups, Spanish-language equivalents (r/spain parenting threads, padres con adolescentes Facebook groups). 1 thoughtful post per week, 3 weeks = 9–15 posts (~1 hour each = 9–15 hours total of authoring + light moderation). Founder authors them — no automation, no spam.
5. **Decision gate at day 21** (anchored to first organic post date): if ≥3 paying parents, the angle has signal — re-evaluate scope. If 0–2 paying parents, the channel mix isn't working — kill paid-ads consideration; do not double down without a clear hypothesis change.

**Why these numbers:** A 3-parent threshold is intentionally low for organic. Organic conversion is small numbers, and the question is "does anyone unrelated to me pay?" — not "is this a scalable channel?" That latter question is for after the threshold is hit.

## Open Questions

1. **Will the Runnymede teacher respond positively to a free-pilot pitch from a former student?** This is the single point of failure for Track 1. If she doesn't respond within 7 days of the message, follow up once, then move to Madrid in-person (Approach C) for Track 1.
2. **Will real students (vs synthetic) give thoughtful free-text answers when assigned by their school?** The product's value depends on rich input. Watch this carefully during the pilot — this is the highest-risk product assumption per the validation doc.
3. **What price will B2C parents convert at?** Open until the ME pricing skill + organic-post experiment produces signal.
4. **Will Runnymede leadership / DPO clear the pilot if the teacher says yes?** Treat teacher-yes as conversation-yes, not pilot-yes. The DPO clearance is a separate gate. If blocked, see Track 1 step 10 fallback.
5. **What happens at the day-21 decision gate if both tracks show signal?** Pre-committed criterion: pick the track with the **higher 12-month expected revenue** at the next milestone, not the closest dollar. A verbal commit for a 2026–27 paid Runnymede cohort at $5/student × 30 students × 8 cohorts = $1,200/year per school, scalable through referrals. 3 paying parents at $49 = $147 total, scalable through more posts. **Tie-break favors Track 1 if it has a verbal commit from any school for the 2026–27 cohort.** Tie-break favors Track 2 if Track 1 has only "interested, will get back to you" as of day 21. The day-21 review surfaces whichever case is real; this rule resolves the call without further analysis.

## Success Criteria

**Track 1 success (realistic base case: fall 2026; upside case: end of June 2026 — Spanish school exam season makes summer pilot the upside, not the default):**
- ✅ One Sixth Form cohort (~20–30 students) uses the product under real conditions
- ✅ Counselor (named, single person — to be identified by Runnymede contact) completes the 15-minute exit interview
- ✅ Founder personally observed at least one student using it (in-person or via screen-share)
- ✅ Founder receives a usable testimonial — defined as: a 2–3 sentence quote attributable to a named counselor/teacher at the school, with explicit permission to use on the Revelio website and in pitch material to other schools (signed email is sufficient)
- ✅ Engagement metric ≥ 70% question completion rate; output-quality metric ≥ 3/5 average from counselor

**Track 1 fail signal:** <40% completion rate, OR counselor exit-interview "would not recommend at any price." Either signals the institutional fit is broken, not the channel.

**Track 2 success (by day 21 of organic-post campaign):**
- ✅ At least 3 paying parents at the chosen price point (deliberately low bar — organic, not scalable channel test)
- ✅ Documented "parent objection" themes from checkout drop-offs and from community thread comments
- ✅ At least one community thread where a parent who is *not* a friend or family member of the founder engages with the post

**Track 2 fail signal:** 0 paying parents AND no positive community-thread engagement after 21 days. Kill the channel mix; do not graduate to paid ads on this signal.

**Track 2 explicit non-criterion:** "More clicks" or "more landing-page views" is not success. Payment is success. Engagement is leading indicator only.

**Both-fail signal:** Pivot back to a deeper problem-discovery loop, not more analysis. The product may be solving a real student problem but not be a fit for either institutional or consumer purchase.

## What I noticed about how you think

- *"we're solving for what do I actually enjoy that I would want to study in college? do i actually need to go to college?"* — you reframed the product space mid-conversation. The validation doc positioned this as "complement to Naviance"; you reframed it as "different problem from Naviance." That's a sharper sentence and it changes who you pitch and how. The reframe came from your own resistance to my "complement is wrong" challenge — which is exactly when reframes are most useful.

That's the same muscle that protects you from your own AI tools generating convincing nonsense. Keep using it. If something I or another AI tells you doesn't match what you've seen with your own eyes, the AI is more likely wrong than your eyes are.
- *"this is something i built for my nieces"* — you reach for the personal anchor first, not the product pitch. That's a sales instinct most engineers don't have. Schools, parents, and investors all respond to personal-anchor openers more than to feature lists.
- *"if she has any feedback/requests on what would actually be useful"* — you defaulted to learning before selling at school #1. The discipline you'll need at school #2 is the opposite: lead with price, not "what would be useful."
