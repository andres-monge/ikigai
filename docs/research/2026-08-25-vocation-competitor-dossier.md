## Executive Assessment

Vocation is the closest known commercial competitor to Revelio. It is not a generic resume optimizer. It starts before the job search, helps a person understand what progress they need from a change, proposes possible roles, and carries that understanding into an action plan and continuing AI coaching.

This materially weakens three broad claims in Revelio's provisional thesis:

- Personalized AI career guidance is already an occupied category.
- Persistent profiles and continuing AI coaching are already part of a competitor's paid offer.
- “Direction before search” is not, by itself, a distinctive position.

The overlap does not invalidate Revelio's central product hypothesis. Vocation's public materials use “prototype” to mean generating, comparing, and investigating possible roles before applying. No public source reviewed for this dossier shows a user performing representative real-world work, returning with observed evidence, and updating a falsifiable career hypothesis across repeated cycles.

The defensible distinction is therefore narrower:

> **Vocation tests role fit through reflection, comparison, and informational interviews. Revelio aims to test person-practice fit by performing representative work and updating from observed evidence.**

That is an unvalidated hypothesis, not yet a moat. The planned longitudinal pilots must establish whether the second conversation after a real experiment produces information and decisions that Vocation does not publicly document.

## Evidence Labels

This dossier distinguishes five kinds of statement:

- **Observed:** directly visible on a current public product or company surface.
- **First-party claim:** stated by Vocation, a founder, or a related official organization.
- **Third-party evidence:** stated by an independent institution or public professional profile.
- **Inference:** a reasoned interpretation of the available evidence.
- **Unknown:** material information not established by the reviewed public sources.

The distinction is especially important for Vocation's research and traction. A credible research lineage is not the same as evidence that the software improves career outcomes.

## Target User and Positioning

### Consumer

**Observed:** The current homepage addresses adults who feel “burned out, stuck, or uncertain” and promises to help them make the right next career move. Its central positioning is **progress before postings**: define what a good change must accomplish before looking at job listings.

The primary context is an adult job switcher with enough work history to discuss:

- what is pushing them away from the present job;
- what is pulling them toward something new;
- which tasks and environments give or drain energy;
- recurring themes and tradeoffs across prior roles; and
- what a better next role should do for them.

### Institutions

**Observed:** The institutional offer targets **adult learners in higher-education and workforce programs**. It promises to improve career readiness and employment outcomes by preparing both the learner and the advisor before a human session.

Learners receive the career-navigation platform. Advisors receive a dashboard containing pushes, pulls, energy drivers, action plans, unresolved issues, suggested questions, and a proposed next move.

The page displays Duet/Southern New Hampshire University, Year Up United, and ASU Local under the wording “Built for institutions like yours.” That wording does not establish that they are paying customers, active pilots, or formal partners.

### Employers and managers

**Observed:** Vocation HQ positions the related employer product around AI interviews, energy analysis, manager coaching, engagement, and turnover risk. Vocation's August 2026 terms also cover employer-sponsored accounts, manager enablement, and employee coaching.

**Inference:** The same underlying career-progress framework can support both sides of a move: helping a person decide whether to leave and helping a manager understand what might make them stay.

## Product Journey

Vocation's public surfaces describe the journey with slightly different taxonomies. The product demo presents five conversational discovery stages; the current homepage turns the later action plan and coach into numbered stages. The exact internal workflow should therefore be treated as unresolved until the product is tested directly.

The combined public journey is:

1. **Career Quest / desired progress.** Explore context, pushes, pulls, anxiety, inertia, and why a change feels necessary now.
2. **Energy profile.** Identify tasks, conditions, and environments that energize or drain the user.
3. **Career themes.** Look across prior work for repeated choices, tradeoffs, needs, and capabilities.
4. **Job prototypes.** Generate and compare possible roles before committing to a job search.
5. **Convergence.** Narrow toward professional fit or a “best job match.”
6. **Action plan.** Turn the selected direction into dated tasks such as informational interviews, resume changes, and interview preparation.
7. **Persistent coach.** Continue asking questions about tradeoffs, storytelling, interviews, networking, and specific opportunities.

The founders' launch material described each discovery conversation as approximately 15–20 minutes. A later public demo described roughly 10–15 exchanges or 20–30 minutes per stage. These timings are marketing guidance, not independently observed completion data.

### What “prototype” appears to mean

The *Job Moves* method uses multiple role prototypes to create contrast. Users compare possible jobs, investigate them through informational interviews and market research, understand the tradeoffs, and then converge on a viable role.

That is a meaningful action beyond a personality label. It is also different from Revelio's proposed representative-work experiment:

| Vocation's publicly described prototype | Revelio's proposed experiment |
|---|---|
| A possible role or job configuration | A falsifiable claim about person–practice fit |
| Compared against other role options | Performed as a small instance of the actual work |
| Investigated through role information and conversations | Evaluated through the user's observed behavior and response |
| Converges toward a job match | Updates, deepens, switches, or replaces a direction |
| Feeds a job-search action plan | Feeds another experiment or a provisional commitment |

This difference is the strongest current basis for differentiation, but product access is required to confirm whether Vocation has capabilities that its marketing does not describe.

## Pricing and Packaging

### Current consumer pricing

**Observed on 2026-08-25:**

| Plan | Price | Publicly listed value |
|---|---:|---|
| Free | $0 | Career Quest, limited coaching interactions, Vocation community |
| Monthly | $39/month | Complete five-step process and unlimited coaching; positioned as a “job-search sprint” |
| Lifetime Access | $179 once | Full process, unlimited coaching, job matches, personalized resources, persistent profile |

At current prices, lifetime access costs the same as approximately 4.6 monthly payments.

Vocation's terms qualify “lifetime” as lasting only while Vocation continues to operate and offer lifetime access.

The packaging maps to different purchase jobs:

- **Free:** “Help me name what is wrong and see whether this is useful.”
- **Monthly:** “Help me through an active, time-bounded job search.”
- **Lifetime:** “Become the career resource I return to across future needs.”

**Inference:** Offering both monthly and lifetime pricing acknowledges that career guidance is episodic. Monthly captures a short period of acute intent; lifetime captures several months of expected value upfront and makes the persistent-profile promise concrete.

An earlier search-indexed Vocation pricing page observed during this research showed a $149 lifetime offer and no monthly plan. That URL now returns 404, so it is not a durable source and the change date is unknown. It is weak evidence that Vocation is actively iterating packaging; current live pricing is authoritative.

### Institutional pricing

**Observed:** Institution licenses are priced custom by cohort size and include:

- full learner access;
- the advisor dashboard;
- onboarding and training for the institution's success team; and
- pilots depending on size and scope.

No public price, minimum cohort, contract length, conversion rate, or case study was found.

### What pricing does and does not prove

Vocation establishes a useful competitive anchor: a serious entrant believes the category can support $39 monthly and $179 one-time consumer pricing, plus custom institutional contracts.

It does **not** establish:

- that either consumer price converts;
- that customers remain active long enough for a subscription;
- that lifetime pricing is economically sustainable;
- that institutions have paid the custom price; or
- that the product improves employment or career-decision outcomes.

Revelio should keep pricing deferred until pilot evidence clarifies the purchase occasion, repeat usage, and outcome being sold.

## Method and Research Lineage

Vocation is built from *Job Moves: 9 Steps for Making Progress in Your Career* by Ethan Bernstein, Michael B. Horn, and Bob Moesta. The book applies Jobs to Be Done theory to careers: a person “hires” a job to make progress in a particular life context.

The framework includes:

- pushes away from the current situation;
- pulls toward a new situation;
- anxiety about the new option;
- inertia or habit preserving the present;
- energy drivers and drains;
- capabilities the person wants to use or develop;
- multiple job prototypes;
- informational interviews and weak ties; and
- explicit tradeoffs rather than an imaginary perfect job.

**First-party claim:** The method is based on more than a decade of research and interviews with more than 1,000 professionals or job switchers. The official book and Vocation surfaces alternate between “over 1,000 interviews,” “over 1,000 professionals,” and “thousands of careers studied.” These statements support a substantial qualitative research origin, but the public product pages do not provide the sampling method, interview protocol, analytic procedure, or product-outcome evaluation.

The most defensible interpretation is:

- The research may support a useful descriptive model of why people change jobs.
- The founders have unusually direct authority to translate that model into a product.
- Neither fact proves that the AI implementation produces better decisions or durable career fit.

Vocation should therefore be treated as a serious **method-and-distribution competitor**, not as proof that the product problem has been solved.

## People Behind Vocation

### Cliff Maxwell — co-founder and CEO

Cliff is the apparent operating and productizing founder. His public biography and launch interview describe a path through CPU engineering, education research and product work, Clayton Christensen's office, Harvard Business School, and early-stage venture investing.

He previously served as Clayton Christensen's Chief of Staff and says he experienced the career-transition research personally before turning the published *Job Moves* method into Vocation.

**Competitive significance:** Cliff connects technical execution, education products, venture judgment, and the Christensen network. He is more credible as the operator of a research-derived product than a founder who merely added an AI interface to a career quiz.

### Michael B. Horn — co-founder

Michael is an Adjunct Lecturer on Education at Harvard Graduate School of Education and co-founder, Distinguished Fellow, and Chairman of the Clayton Christensen Institute. He has written extensively about education, workforce change, and helping people build their passions and potential.

**Competitive significance:** Michael contributes research legitimacy, publishing and content distribution, investor access, and relationships with education and workforce organizations. His involvement makes the institutional route more credible.

### Bob “Bobby” Moesta — co-founder

Bob is the founder of The Re-Wired Group and one of the principal architects of Jobs to Be Done. His firm says he has helped create or launch more than 3,500 products, services, and businesses.

**Competitive significance:** Bob provides direct methodological lineage and deep product-development practice. Vocation can credibly say its product comes from the authors and practitioners behind the method rather than from a superficial reading of it.

### Ethan Bernstein — research lineage, not company team

Ethan is the third *Job Moves* co-author and was an organizational-behavior professor at Harvard Business School during the research and publication of the book. He is not listed as a Vocation founder or team member.

**Competitive significance:** His role strengthens the academic and workplace-research lineage. It should not be represented as company involvement without further evidence.

### Engineering and operating depth

Vocation's LinkedIn profile currently lists one employee and only Cliff. Varun Bachalli's public portfolio separately says he has worked part-time for Pathfinder Products Corp since February 2025 as the sole engineer across Vocation and Vocation HQ. He self-reports use by more than 100 employees and candidates across three B2B pilots.

That claim is useful but not independently verified, and Varun is not present on Vocation's official team page.

**Inference:** Public evidence points to a lean company centered on Cliff, supported by high-authority co-founders with substantial outside roles and at least one part-time engineer. This creates key-person and execution risk, while also showing that a small team can cover a surprisingly broad product surface.

## Distribution and Trust Architecture

Vocation combines several reinforcing trust and acquisition assets:

- the *Job Moves* book and its existing audience;
- the Christensen and Jobs to Be Done lineage;
- Michael Horn's education and workforce platform;
- Bob Moesta's practitioner reputation;
- a free two-minute quiz and free first stage;
- testimonials from named professionals;
- a newsletter and career-content library;
- an institutional demo route; and
- an adjacent employer product.

This is a stronger commercial package than “AI career coach” alone. Its strongest visible commercial advantage is the combination of a named method, credible people, understandable outputs, and multiple routes to users.

The public evidence remains weak on outcomes:

- no consumer count;
- no paying-customer count;
- no conversion or retention data;
- no comparative product evaluation;
- no longitudinal career outcomes;
- no institutional case studies; and
- no named or quantified funding round.

The current privacy policy is also brief relative to the sensitivity and institutional sharing described in the newer terms. The policy says data is not sold or shared without consent. The terms say organization-sponsored accounts may expose reports, insights, progress indicators, or coaching outputs to authorized representatives. Those statements can be reconciled through consent and program-specific disclosure, but the public explanation is not yet detailed.

Vocation normally requires users to be at least 18 unless it expressly permits otherwise. This reduces direct overlap with a youth-first Revelio offer but does not remove the need for stronger privacy and safeguarding if Revelio returns to the school market.

## Strategic Implications for Revelio

### Preserve

- Keep the longitudinal pilot focused on whether action creates decision-relevant information.
- Keep representative-work fidelity as a core design constraint.
- Preserve observations, prior hypotheses, and what changed between sessions.
- Keep the user's judgment central.
- Continue refusing fit scores and confidence percentages that imply validation the product does not have.

### Revise

- Qualify the broad “orphaned process” claim: Vocation commercially occupies substantial pre-search guidance territory, while the representative-work evidence loop remains unproven and apparently unowned.
- Treat Vocation as a named direct competitor, not as another generic AI job-search tool.
- Stop presenting personalization, continuity, memory, or direction-before-search as unique.
- State uniqueness as an evidence question: no reviewed public competitor demonstrates the complete representative-work and hypothesis-update loop.

### Do not copy yet

- Job matching.
- A content and resource portal.
- Community.
- Institution dashboards.
- Manager and retention tools.
- Subscription and lifetime billing.
- Branded *Job Moves* concepts or workflow terminology.

These may become useful later, but copying the breadth now would avoid the central validation question.

### Test

The pilot should determine whether Revelio's proposed difference is real:

1. Does a representative experiment reveal information unavailable from reflection and role comparison?
2. Does the return conversation materially change or strengthen the next decision?
3. Do users want another experiment rather than treating the first answer or action plan as the endpoint?
4. Can users explain their decision using evidence from what they did?
5. Does preserving evidence across loops create value beyond a persistent conversational profile?

A later hands-on competitive test should take one consistent persona through both products, with permission and a dedicated test account, and compare:

- time and friction to first useful insight;
- depth and adaptability of questioning;
- diversity and grounding of proposed directions;
- what “prototype” asks the user to do;
- how uncertainty and tradeoffs are represented;
- whether any output claims unsupported precision;
- what happens after the action plan;
- what the product remembers on return; and
- where the paywall appears.

## Documentation Consequences

This dossier should remain standalone because it records a different, fast-changing evidence type from the August 11 method synthesis.

The August documents are explicitly historical snapshots. Rewriting their conclusions would erase the fact that Vocation was missed. If backward discoverability becomes necessary, append only a clearly labeled “Later research” link rather than merging this content into their original analysis.

The provisional thesis should be updated where Vocation changes current claims. The active implementation plan should cite this dossier as a research input without expanding scope or moving pricing ahead of pilot evidence.

## Unknowns Worth Monitoring

1. Consumer conversion, retention, revenue, and active-user counts.
2. Whether the $39 and $179 prices have produced meaningful demand.
3. The identity and status of institutional customers or pilots.
4. Whether the advisor dashboard is live and used in practice.
5. Whether Vocation performs repeated post-action observation and adaptation inside the product.
6. Whether job matches use current labor-market data and how fit is calculated.
7. How percentages such as energy-driver scores are produced and validated.
8. How organization-sponsored data visibility and consent work in product.
9. The operating roles and time commitment of Michael Horn and Bob Moesta.
10. Funding, ownership, runway, and hiring plans.
