Your Why I Work — confirmed

I work so that people who depend on complicated systems don’t have to become experts just to get what they need. Most of that complexity was somebody’s choice, and nobody went back to revisit it.

The same week, three ways

What each path would actually contain, including the flat parts. These are ways of working, not job titles, and none is being suggested over the others.

Teaching the people the system was built for

You stop translating complexity for one company and start making other people able to translate it for themselves.

Tue

You spend the evening cutting your slides in half. The version you were proud of assumed people knew what a database was, and the six people coming on Thursday do not.

Thu

Ninety minutes with six people at a nonprofit, on the tool they already hate. Two of them get it in the first twenty minutes. One is on her phone. You find out that the thing you thought was the hard part isn’t.

Sat

You rewrite the session from scratch based on where they actually got stuck. Nobody has paid you anything, and you notice you’re looking forward to running it again.

What that week still wouldn’t tell you

Do you like teaching, or do you like being the person who knows?

Where it could go

Work that pays by the cohort rather than by the year, and material that keeps earning after you’ve stopped writing it.

Why it might already fit

You’ve rewritten onboarding docs three times unasked. The runbook you wrote in 2023 is still what people send to new hires.

Fit with your life

€600–1,400 a day once established, but the first year is mostly unpaid audience-building. Evenings and one weekday are enough to test it. No credential required.

Building the narrow tool that deletes a step

Instead of explaining the complicated thing, you remove the part of it that never needed to exist.

Sat

Two hours in the back office of a veterinary practice, watching a receptionist retype the same appointment into two systems. You are not writing code. You are watching, and it is slower than you expected.

Sun

You build the ugliest possible version of the one thing that wastes the most time. It has no login and no styling. It works for exactly one clinic.

Wed

She uses it twice and then goes back to the old way, because yours doesn’t handle the case where the owner changes the pet’s name. You fix it that night. Nobody else knows this exists.

What that week still wouldn’t tell you

Can you stay interested in someone else’s boring problem long enough to finish?

Where it could go

A product with maybe 200 customers that one person can maintain, in a trade you’d otherwise never have met.

Why it might already fit

The scripts you wrote for your own team are the only side work you kept using after you stopped being paid to.

Fit with your life

€2–5k monthly recurring in 18–30 months, well under your income for at least two years. The only one of the three you can run without leaving your job.

Working upstream, on how the systems get chosen

The complexity you keep absorbing was bought by someone in a meeting. You go to the meeting.

Mon

You read a live public tender for a records system, 140 pages, most of it boilerplate. Around page 60 you find a requirement that guarantees the thing will be unusable, and you understand exactly why it’s there.

Wed

You write the teardown. It takes three evenings, and cutting it from 4,000 words to 900 is harder than writing it. You have to keep taking the contempt out.

Fri

You send it to three people who work in that space. One replies with a single line: “page 60 is deliberate.” The other two don’t reply at all. Nothing about the tender changes.

What that week still wouldn’t tell you

Can you tolerate a pace where a good outcome takes two years and has your name nowhere on it?

Where it could go

Influence over what an entire agency or hospital group makes people use, rather than over one codebase.

Why it might already fit

You already do this informally. The vendor you argued against in 2024 was dropped, and you read procurement threads for fun.

Fit with your life

€55–75k in your city, a real cut from your package. Entry usually runs through a fixed-term contract or a fellowship. Most people arrive from engineering, not policy.

Rewriting or replacing a path changes only that path, and changes nothing about the other two. Nothing here is chosen until you start one.
