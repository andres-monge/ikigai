Your Why I Work — confirmed

I work so that people who depend on complicated systems don’t have to become experts just to get what they need. Most of that complexity was somebody’s choice, and nobody went back to revisit it.

Three ways to serve that

Read across a row to compare the same question three ways. Each one is a different way of working, not a job title.

The path

Teaching the people the system was built for

Building the narrow tool that deletes a step

Working upstream, on how the systems get chosen

Serves your Why

TeachingYou stop translating complexity for one company and start making other people able to translate it for themselves.

BuildingInstead of explaining the complicated thing, you remove the part of it that never needed to exist.

UpstreamThe complexity you keep absorbing was bought by someone in a meeting. You go to the meeting.

Could become

TeachingWork that pays by the cohort rather than by the year, and material that keeps earning after you’ve stopped writing it.

BuildingA product with maybe 200 customers that one person can maintain, in a trade you’d otherwise never have met.

UpstreamInfluence over what an entire agency or hospital group makes people use, rather than over one codebase.

Evidence it may fit

TeachingYou’ve rewritten onboarding docs three times without being asked. The runbook you wrote in 2023 is still what people send to new hires.

BuildingThe scripts you wrote for your own team are the only side work you kept using after you stopped being paid to.

UpstreamYou already do this informally. The vendor you argued against in 2024 was dropped, and you read procurement threads for fun.

The central unknown

TeachingDo you like teaching, or do you like being the person who knows?

BuildingCan you stay interested in someone else’s boring problem long enough to finish?

UpstreamCan you tolerate a pace where a good outcome takes two years and has your name nowhere on it?

First project

TeachingRun one 90-minute session for six people at a local nonprofit, on the tool they already hate. Charge nothing. Watch where they get stuck.

BuildingSpend two weekends at one veterinary practice watching their intake process, then ship the ugliest possible version of the one thing that wastes the most time.

UpstreamWrite a public teardown of one live public-sector tender for a system you actually understand. Send it to three people who work in that space.

Fit with your life

TeachingIndependent instructors here bill €600–1,400 a day, but the first year is mostly unpaid audience-building. Evenings and one weekday would be enough to test it. No credential required; the buyers are ops managers, not L&D.

BuildingVertical micro-SaaS typically reaches €2–5k monthly recurring in 18–30 months — well under your current income for at least two years. The only one of the three you can run without leaving your job. Distribution is the constraint, not code.

UpstreamCivic and public-sector roles in your city pay €55–75k, a real cut from your current package. Entry usually runs through a fixed-term contract or a fellowship, and most people arrive from engineering rather than policy.

Act on one

Rewriting or replacing a path changes only that path, and changes nothing about the other two. Nothing here is chosen until you start one.
