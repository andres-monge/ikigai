**Tester:** Maya Chen

**Test Date:** 2025-12-26

### Overall Grade: **Good**

### Dimension-by-Dimension Evaluation

#### 1. Problem Recognition: **Good** (Not Outstanding)

**What Worked:**
> "Your purpose lies in the intersection of your intolerance for poorly designed systems and your natural gift for making the complex feel manageable and fair for others."

> "The world tells seventeen-year-olds they have to buy a $200,000 ticket to a game where the rules change every week. You see how unfair this is."

> "You can see through the 'college or failure' false dichotomy."

The app clearly understood I'm a high schooler dealing with college pressure. It acknowledged the financial anxiety and the "college or failure" trap.

**The Gap:**
- Doesn't explicitly validate that "not having a passion" is normal at 17
- Doesn't acknowledge AI anxiety about creative careers
- Assumes I already have strong opinions about "unfair systems" - puts words in my mouth
- Missing: "It's okay to not know at 17"

---

#### 2. Constraint Respect: **Outstanding**

**What Worked:**
> "Calculate the 'Total Cost of Ownership' for both, including four years of interest on a standard student loan."

> "You can name three specific career paths where a $5,000 investment yields the same starting salary as a $120,000 degree."

> "Create a 'Parent Persuasion Script' that uses your ROI data to answer the 'but you need a degree' argument."

This is the strongest part of the app. It directly addresses the financial "donut hole" reality - too rich for aid, too poor to pay. The action plan shows paths that don't require expensive degrees, validates community college as strategic (not failure), and even gives me a script to convince my parents. The salary ranges ($65k-$95k) feel realistic, not inflated.

---

#### 3. Actionability: **Good** (Not Outstanding)

**What Works:**
> "Today, find the exact tuition and fees for your local community college and the nearest four-year state university."

> "Create a simple spreadsheet comparing the hours of work required at a median entry-level wage to pay off both options."

**What Doesn't:**
> "Find three local employers in high-growth fields and ask their hiring managers which certifications they actually value over a degree."

> "Search LinkedIn for people in your area who have the job you want but didn't go to a prestigious school; message them to ask how they did it."

The first milestone ("Expose the Math") is actually doable - I could create that spreadsheet during study hall. But Week 3 wants me to cold-message hiring managers and LinkedIn strangers? I've never done that. I don't know what to say.

**The Gap:**
Steps escalate too quickly from "research on my laptop" to "network with adults." No scaffolding for how a socially anxious 17-year-old actually does informational interviews.

**Suggested fix:** "This week (15 min): Ask your school counselor which local employers recruit from your school. Write down 3 names."

---

#### 4. Recommendation Likelihood: **Good** (Not Outstanding)

**Shareable Moments:**
> "The world tells seventeen-year-olds they have to buy a $200,000 ticket to a game where the rules change every week."

The "Architect of Alternative Trajectories" framing is cool - it makes being undecided feel strategic instead of broken.

**Why Not Outstanding:**
- Doesn't address the AI fear we all have
- Action plan feels like homework, not hope
- Missing the reframe: "Choosing isn't forever - it's just choosing your first experiment"

---

**What didn't:**
- Some questions felt too big: "What meaningful change would you be proud you helped create in 10 years?" I can't even picture next year.
- "Show Me My Purpose" button feels intense - promising an answer I don't think exists

---

#### Results Quality

**What didn't:**
- Assumes I already have strong convictions about "dismantling opaque systems" - I'm just stressed about college
- Tone is intense and dramatic: "You derive meaning from building systems" - do I?

**What didn't:**
- By Week 3, wants me to contact hiring managers - I freeze up talking to cashiers
- "Find one person who is currently paralyzed by college debt fear" - that's literally everyone I know, but how do I "walk them through my map" when I'm still paralyzed myself?
- Timeline (12 weeks) feels both too long and too short while finishing senior year

### Recommendations for Outstanding Rating

#### 1. Explicitly Address the AI Fear
Add a section in results:
> "You mentioned AI killed your interest in digital art. Here's the thing: AI is a tool, not a replacement for your judgment. The paths we've identified require human empathy and organizational thinking - skills AI can't replicate."

#### 2. Validate the "Passionless" Feeling
Before showing paths, include:
> "You said you don't have a 'passion' - and that's actually fine. Most adults change careers 5-7 times. The goal isn't to choose perfectly at 17. It's to pick something reasonable to start, then iterate."

#### 3. Scaffold the Scary Steps
For networking actions, add micro-steps:
> "Week 3, Day 1 (10 min): Don't contact anyone yet. Just find 3 LinkedIn profiles of people who work in career counseling in your area. Notice what their job titles are. That's it for today."

#### 4. Add a "First Step This Week" Section
At the top of the action plan:
> "Overwhelmed? Start here: This week during study hall, spend 15 minutes opening your notes app. Write down 3 things about the college process that frustrate you most. Don't solve them - just name them."

#### 5. Make Community College Feel Strategic
Add explicit validation:
> "Here's something your counselor probably didn't tell you: Community college transfers have the same degree as 4-year freshmen, but save $30-50k and have time to figure out their major without the debt clock ticking."

#### 6. Address the "What If I Choose Wrong" Fear
Include in the ikigai summary:
> "These aren't 'forever' paths. They're experiments. If you try one for 6 months and hate it, you'll know more about what you DON'T want - and that's valuable data, not failure."
